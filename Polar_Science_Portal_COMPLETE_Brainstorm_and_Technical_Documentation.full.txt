0. Original Product Brainstorm  Preserve the Full Intent
The portal should be a comprehensive polar-science outreach and knowledge system rather than a generic document website. It should
archive expedition reports, scientific datasets, research, publications, photographs, videos and institutional activities while also turning that
knowledge into searchable, understandable and reusable public-facing content.
 Home must remain clutter-free and act as a discovery/intelligence dashboard rather than exposing every feature at once.
 Sidebar must expose Social Media Posts, Research Connection, Institutional Activities and AI as clear sections.
 Semantic Search should be prominent in the top navigation/header because it is a core USP.
 AI Data Trends should appear on Home as a visual intelligence section.
 Knowledge Graph should have a Home preview and a dedicated full-page experience.
 Notifications should be a top-right icon and include newly generated posts, newly added reports, data changes, new publications, new
datasets and institutional activities.
 Login and Signup should be visible when logged out; after login they should become a profile icon.
 The portal should have a complete Polar Knowledge Repository covering expedition reports, scientific datasets, research, publications,
photographs, reports, conferences, seminars, workshops and awareness events.
 Search and filtering must work across repository content.
 Reports should support AI summarization.
 The platform should generate social media posts from scientific content.
 USP features are Knowledge Graph, Research Connection and Semantic Search.
 Recent uploaded data should be visible both as a Home feature and through notifications.
 The Smart Education theme should be represented through a student-friendly learning experience and simplified scientific explanations.
1. Product Goals
 Centralize polar scientific knowledge in one searchable repository.
 Make scientific content discoverable through both conventional and semantic search.
 Connect researchers, institutions, expeditions, publications, datasets and locations.
 Use AI to summarize, classify, connect and transform scientific content without removing human review.
 Turn repository content into outreach material for websites and social platforms.
 Provide trend intelligence from accumulated repository data.
 Make complex polar science accessible to students and the public.
 Maintain scientific provenance, source attribution and content/version history.
 Provide an administration layer for ingestion, moderation, approvals and analytics.
2. Target User Groups
 Students: discover simple educational polar-science resources, reports, videos and quizzes.
 Researchers: search publications, datasets, reports, expeditions, researchers and institutions.
 Scientific institutions: publish and organize reports, datasets, research and activities.
 Educators: find reliable content and convert scientific material into teaching resources.
 General public: explore polar science through simplified explanations, media and outreach.
 Content/Admin teams: manage repository records, uploads, metadata, AI-generated content and moderation.
 Outreach/Social teams: generate, review, schedule and publish platform-specific scientific communication.

4. Home Page  Detailed Brainstorm
 Header: logo, primary navigation, prominent semantic search, notification icon, login/signup or profile icon.
 Hero: short mission statement about polar science knowledge and outreach plus a large natural-language search box.
 Search examples: 'Find Antarctic sea-ice research', 'Show reports from recent expeditions', 'Researchers working on polar biodiversity'.
 Quick Access: Expeditions, Reports, Datasets, Research, Publications, Photographs, Videos, Conferences, Seminars, Workshops and
Awareness Events.
 Recent Uploads: latest repository additions with content type, title, institution, date and actions.
 AI Data Trends: research growth, publication activity, expedition activity, dataset growth, frequent topics and emerging areas.
 Knowledge Graph Preview: a compact interactive graph showing relationships among researchers, institutions, expeditions, publications
and datasets.
 Featured Research: curated or algorithmically relevant research and publications.
 Upcoming Activities: next conferences, seminars, workshops and outreach programs.
 Featured Expedition: visual card with location, dates, objectives and related resources.

8. Search & Filtering
 Global semantic search should search across reports, datasets, publications, research, expeditions and media metadata.
 Natural-language queries should retrieve conceptually related content even when exact keywords differ.
 Filters: content type, Arctic/Antarctic, year, expedition, institution, research domain, author/researcher and event type.
 Sorting: relevance, newest, oldest and optionally popularity/view count.
 Search results should show title, type, short snippet, relevance score, source and quick actions.
 Search suggestions should be generated from indexed titles, entities and common terms.
 Recent searches should be stored for authenticated users.
 Search results should link directly to source documents/resources.
9. AI Data Trends
 Research activity trend by year/month.
 Publication growth over time.
 Dataset additions and updates.
 Expedition activity by region/year.
 Research domain distribution.
 Frequent scientific topics and keywords.
 Emerging topics detected from recent content.
 Arctic versus Antarctic content distribution.
 Institutional research activity distribution.
 AI-generated narrative insight explaining observed repository patterns.
 Trend outputs should be treated as analytical summaries, with source data and calculation period visible.
10. Knowledge Graph  Full Brainstorm
 Entities: Researcher, Institution, Expedition, Location, Research Project, Report, Dataset, Publication, Media, Topic and Event.
 Relationships: works-at, authored, participated-in, studies, produced, uses-dataset, related-to, located-at and organized-by.
 Node click should show metadata and connected resources.
 Graph filters should include entity type, region, year and research domain.
 Graph search should allow a researcher, institution, topic or expedition to become the focus node.
 For the MVP, construct nodes and edges from PostgreSQL relationships.
 Neo4j can be introduced later if graph traversal becomes a genuine scale requirement.

17. Database Decision  PostgreSQL
 Primary database: PostgreSQL.
 Reason: the system has strong structured relationships among users, researchers, institutions, expeditions, reports, datasets, publications,
events and media.
 Use normalized relational tables and explicit relationship tables where many-to-many connections exist.
 Use PostgreSQL constraints, indexes and transactions for reliable scientific metadata and administrative workflows.
 Use pgvector for embeddings and semantic similarity search in the initial system.
 Do not introduce MongoDB simply because some AI content is semi-structured; JSON/JSONB fields in PostgreSQL can handle flexible
metadata where appropriate.
 Do not introduce Neo4j at the MVP stage unless graph complexity justifies it.
 Dataset versioning is separate from API versioning and should be supported if datasets evolve.
18. File Storage
 Store PDFs, videos, photographs and large datasets in object storage rather than PostgreSQL binary columns.
 Possible storage: Amazon S3, Cloudflare R2, Supabase Storage, Azure Blob or MinIO.
 PostgreSQL stores file metadata, object key/URL, MIME type, size, source, owner, timestamps and relationships.
 Use signed URLs or controlled download endpoints where access restrictions apply.
19. AI Service Decision
 Create a separate logical Python FastAPI AI service.
 Do not make it an unnecessarily complex microservice ecosystem for the MVP.
 Node/Express remains the application backend and API gateway.
 Python handles LLM orchestration, embeddings, RAG, document processing, metadata extraction, entity extraction and trend analysis.
 Frontend never calls the Python AI service directly.
 Recommended flow: React 
®
 Node/Express 
®
 Python FastAPI AI Service 
®
 LLM/Embeddings/Vector Layer.
 This separation allows the AI stack to evolve independently without exposing provider credentials or internal AI endpoints to the browser.

22. Asynchronous AI Job Pattern
 POST /api/ai/summarize should create a job for large documents instead of waiting for completion.
 Return HTTP 202 with a jobId and processing status.
 GET /api/ai/jobs/:jobId should return queued, processing, completed or failed status.
 On completion, store the result and create a notification.
 Redis + BullMQ can be introduced for reliable background job processing when required.
23. Backend API Convention
 Use unversioned API links as requested: /api/reports, not /api/v1/reports.
 Use plural REST resource names.
 Use :id for individual resource paths.
 Use query parameters for filtering, sorting, pagination and search options.
 Do not create separate routes for every possible filter.
 Keep authentication and authorization in middleware.
 Keep validation at API boundaries.
 Use consistent error response structure.
 Paginate repository lists and search results.

28. Frontend Architecture Guidance
 Create reusable layout components: AppShell, Sidebar, Header, NotificationBell, ProfileMenu and SearchBar.
 Create reusable content components: ReportCard, DatasetCard, PublicationCard, ExpeditionCard, ResearcherCard, InstitutionCard and
MediaCard.
 Create reusable data states: Loading, EmptyState, ErrorState, Pagination and FilterBar.
 Use a consistent design system for typography, spacing, cards, badges, buttons, tables, charts and graph controls.
 Keep scientific content readable and avoid dashboard-style visual overload.
 Use progressive disclosure: show summary first, details on demand.
 Make important source/provenance information visible without dominating the design.

 Frontend: React or Next.js.
 Main backend: Node.js + Express.
 Database: PostgreSQL.
 Vector search: pgvector.
 AI service: Python + FastAPI.
 LLM: external provider behind an internal abstraction layer.
 File storage: S3/R2/Supabase Storage/Azure Blob/MinIO.
 Background jobs: Redis + BullMQ when asynchronous workloads require it.
 Knowledge Graph visualization: React Flow or Cytoscape.js.
 Charts: a React-compatible charting library.
 Authentication: secure cookie/session strategy or short-lived access credentials with refresh mechanism.
 Deployment can use Docker so Node, Python AI service and supporting services are reproducible.
30. What Not to Overengineer Initially
 Do not use MongoDB + PostgreSQL together without a concrete requirement.
 Do not add Neo4j only because a Knowledge Graph exists; PostgreSQL relationships are sufficient for an MVP.
 Do not add a separate vector database when pgvector is sufficient for the initial scale.
 Do not expose the Python AI service directly to the browser.
 Do not make every AI operation synchronous.
 Do not allow generated social content to bypass human approval.
 Do not create API versions in URLs because the agreed convention is unversioned /api/resource paths.
 Do not store large PDFs/videos/images directly in PostgreSQL.

39. Final Decisions to Lock
 Database: PostgreSQL.
 Vector search: pgvector.
 API URLs: unversioned /api/... paths.
 Main backend: Node.js + Express.
 AI processing: logically separate Python + FastAPI service.
 Frontend communicates with Node, not directly with AI service.
 Files: object storage, not PostgreSQL binary storage.
 Knowledge Graph: PostgreSQL relationships initially.
 AI workflow: source-grounded + human review for public-facing output.
 Notifications: centralized event/notification model.
 Home: clean discovery/intelligence page.
 Sidebar: organized into Main, Knowledge, Outreach, Intelligence and User.
 Smart Education: dedicated learning mode connected to primary scientific sources.
